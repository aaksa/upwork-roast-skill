# Upwork facts the roast relies on

Checked against Upwork's Help Center, Upwork's resources pages, academic studies and labelled third-party data on **2026-10-13**. The full sourced report is `research/upwork-roast-research-2026.md` in the skill's repository. Upwork edits these pages often, and some disagree with each other. Wherever a limit is disputed, say so in one line and tell the user to confirm it in their own profile editor.

Evidence tags: **[Upwork]** Upwork's own pages · **[Academic]** peer-reviewed or working papers · **[Third-party]** tool vendors and guides (weaker) · **[Anecdote]**.

## How clients decide

Clients judge three surfaces in order:

1. **The search card** [Upwork]: photo, title, hourly rate, only the top badge, the "Available now" badge, and only about the first 250 characters of the overview. Clients in Upwork's Uma test also see "AI insights", an AI summary built from the description, skills, work history and client feedback. Clients can run a title-only search, so a title without the words they type can be missed.
2. **The proposal row** [Upwork]: photo, title, rate, Job Success Score, badges, earnings and top skills, next to the first one or two sentences of the cover letter. The top 4 slots go to Boosted proposals.
3. **The opened proposal or full profile** [Upwork]: since May 28, 2026 there is one profile. Specialized Profiles are gone, and Upwork reorders work history, reviews, portfolio and skills so the items most relevant to the client's search or job post come first. The title and overview do not reorder; they are the same for every client.

**Uma (Upwork's AI)** reads the profile before many clients do. Uma Recruiter shortlists freelancers for clients, even on the Basic plan. It weighs the profile description, demonstrated expertise, past project performance, portfolio depth, availability and JSS, plus the proposal text and screening answers [Upwork, engineering blog, May 2026]. How it weighs text against tags and reviews is not published. "Write so the AI can quote you" is reasoned advice, not a tested fact.

What moves hiring, from the studies:
- **On-platform track record matters most.** A first hire roughly tripled newcomers' later earnings (Pallais, AER 2014). Reviews in the same category as the job predict performance better than the overall score (Kokkodis). Rating stars are inflated, so the review text matters more than the stars (Filippas, Horton & Golden 2022). [Academic]
- **Clear, error-free writing is a hiring lever.** Grammar-only help on profile text raised hires about 8% and wages about 10% across ~500,000 jobseekers (Wiles, Munyikwa & Horton, Management Science 2025). [Academic]
- **A coherent career beats a scattered one.** Freelancers with related, not identical, jobs were hired more than those with scattered histories (Leung, ASR 2014). [Academic]
- **Since LLMs, tailored-sounding letters have lost most of their value.** Employers lean more on reputation and hard-to-fake proof (Galdin & Silbert 2025; Cui, Dias & Ye 2025). In Cui, Dias & Ye, more than 75% of AI letters were sent within a minute, and freelancers who spent longer editing the draft got more offers. [Academic]
- **Upwork's own warning (2026):** relying too much on AI drafts is "a growing reason clients discard applications". When every proposal sounds the same, clients stop reading after the first sentence. Red flags Upwork names: "Dear Hiring Manager", "I'm thrilled", "robust solution", "delve into", "I am excited to apply". [Upwork]
- **People can't reliably detect AI text**, but they punish text that reads generic, impersonal and template-shaped. Cosmetic "human" touches (contractions, typos) don't help; specifics do (Jakesch et al., PNAS 2023; Packard & Berger on concrete language). [Academic]
- **Newcomers and non-US freelancers:** clients discount workers from less developed countries, and verified work history and good reputation shrink the penalty. Time-zone gaps hurt even with a good reputation (Agrawal et al.; Hong & Pavlou). [Academic]

## Field limits (verify in the editor)

| Field | What to use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Title | Aim for 50 characters or fewer and 10 words or fewer; hard cap 70 | Upwork says "10 words or less" and older Upwork pages say 70; one 2026 vendor reports 50; the current help center gives no number |
| Overview | The first ~250 characters show in search | [Upwork] essentials article |
| Skills | 15 ranked core skills, plus up to 5 extra marked "add if your editor allows 20" | The essentials article (edited 2026-09-30) says 15; the skills article (edited 2026-07-06) says 20 |
| Portfolio title | 40 characters or fewer | Portfolio Guide (Jul 2026) says 40; the help center (edited 2026-10-07) says 70 |
| Portfolio description | First 300 characters must stand alone; 600 or fewer in total | Portfolio Guide says under 300; the help center says 600 |
| Portfolio role / tags | Role up to 100 characters; up to 5 skill tags per item | [Upwork] |
| Portfolio count | 3–5 strong items per main service | [Upwork] Portfolio Guide |

## Rules that are not style

- **No contact details anywhere** [Upwork]: email, phone, Skype, Telegram, WhatsApp, Slack, social handles, Calendly or "visit my site", including inside portfolio files and linked sites. Breaking this can mean restrictions, losing badges, or losing the account.
- **No false or misleading claims** [Upwork, Terms of Use]. Badges: Top Rated is the top 10%, Top Rated Plus the top 3%, Expert-Vetted the top 1% (visible only to Business Plus and Enterprise clients). Claiming a tier you don't hold is misleading.
- **No other people's work, and no ads for services outside Upwork.** Sample or mock-up projects are fine if labelled [Upwork].
- **AI is allowed.** Upwork has no blanket ban on AI-written profiles or proposals. The Terms ban lying about experience, and ban AI-bolstered proposals only where the client restricts AI. The freelancer is responsible for every word. No primary source says Upwork runs AI-text detection.
- **Photo:** a real, clear headshot of the freelancer. No sunglasses, logos, clip-art, group photos or digitally altered images [Upwork].
- **Visibility:** profiles turn private automatically for new freelancers with no proposals in their first 90 days, or no earnings within 90 days of the first proposal, and for established freelancers after two or more years without earnings [Upwork]. If the user says they get no views, check this first.
- **Paid levers buy placement, not persuasion.** The Availability Badge does not change search rank; it shows as a badge and a filter. Boosting buys a top slot; in one field experiment about 80% of its effect came from position (Filippas et al.). Quote Upwork's "up to 70% more invites", "17% more likely to be seen" and "up to 24% more hires" only as Upwork's own claims, which give no method.

## Proposals

- **Length** [Upwork]: about 200–300 words or fewer, or three short paragraphs. Vendor data (GigRadar, 133,872 agency proposals) shows a U shape, with very short and long proof-led letters doing better than the middle. It is not causal. Default to 120–250 words; under 80 for simple jobs.
- **The first one or two sentences show in the client's list** [Upwork]. They must name the client's problem and a matching proof.
- **Applying early** has the strongest support (Stanton & Thomas, oDesk). GigRadar's data peaks at 3–4 minutes after posting, but it is vendor data with gaps. Never quote the "2–4 hours is best" claim, which is unverified.
- **Ranking** [Upwork]: profile and proposal are matched against the job post, keywords included, and bids outside the budget are listed lower. Uma also scores the proposal text and screening answers.
- **Other Upwork advice:** greet by name if known, never "To Whom It May Concern"; include any code word the job asks for; end with a specific next step (a short call or a paid test). Proposals can be edited for 6 hours or until the client views them. Connects cost $0.15 each.
