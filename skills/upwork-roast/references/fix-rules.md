# Fix rules

These rules come from `upwork-facts-2026.md`. The short version: **proof, not polish.** Fluent, tailored-sounding copy is now free to produce and clients discount it. What still wins is concrete, checkable proof in the few characters clients actually see. The rewrite's job is to dig the real specifics out of the profile, put them where they're seen, and invent nothing.

## Voice: sound like the freelancer on a good day

1. **Edit, don't replace.** Start from the freelancer's own text. Keep their nouns, client types, project names and good sentences word for word. Fix clarity, order and length, not their voice. In the research, help that only corrected and clarified writing raised hires about 8% (Wiles et al. 2025). Upwork's own test is to read it aloud and check it "sounds like you".
2. **Human means specific, not chatty.** Make copy human with named client types, named tools, real numbers, one trade-off and varied sentence length. Never fake it with added contractions, typos, jokes or "Hey there!".
3. **Business first.** Lead with what the client gets: a launched app, users, revenue, funding, time saved. Name the tools only where they prove the result.
4. **Plain English, zero errors.** Short sentences, everyday words. Proofread every line; errors cost hires.
5. **No symbols as structure.** Upwork advises against symbols and "laundry list" bullets. Remove emoji headings, star ratings, ✔ walls, ALL CAPS, decorative lines and bold section headers. A short bullet list is fine only if every bullet carries a concrete example or number.
6. **No hype.** No scarcity ("only 1 slot left"), no superlatives ("best", "top"), no badges the freelancer doesn't hold.

### Words and patterns that make copy read as machine-written

Flag these in the roast (reason: **sounds AI-written**) and never put them in a rewrite. One hit isn't proof; flag the pattern when there are several.

- **Words** (the strongest research support is for the first six): delve, underscore, showcase, pivotal, crucial, intricate, tapestry, testament, vibrant, landscape, realm, garner, foster, robust, seamless, leverage, elevate, cutting-edge, game-changer, results-driven, passionate, dedicated, motivated team player.
- **Phrases Upwork names as red flags:** "Dear Hiring Manager", "To Whom It May Concern", "I'm thrilled", "I am excited to apply", "robust solution", "delve into", "I have read your job description".
- **Structures:** "not just X, but Y" / "It's not X, it's Y"; everything in threes; "serves as / stands as / a testament to"; trailing "-ing" clauses ("…, ensuring quality"); openers like "In today's fast-paced world"; summary closers ("In short, …"); keyword lists; the same claim repeated in different words.
- **Em dashes:** a weak signal only. Keep them rare; never call text AI-written for em dashes alone.

## Never invent

- Use only the fact sheet: no new numbers, results, client names, review quotes, years, tools, certifications or badges.
- A number can be moved or shortened, never changed or rounded up. "Worked on" never becomes "built".
- Where a fix needs a fact the freelancer hasn't given, write `[ADD: what is needed]`, for example `[ADD: app name]` or `[ADD: how many users at launch]`. A plain directional result the freelancer confirms ("cut load time noticeably") beats no result.

## 1. Three titles

- Service noun first: the exact words a client types into search ("Flutter Developer", "Shopify Expert", "Bookkeeper"). Some clients search titles only.
- Then one qualifier: the client type or the result. At most one `|`.
- Aim for 50 characters or fewer and 10 words or fewer; never over 70. Count with a script and show the count. Add one line saying Upwork's sources disagree on the limit, so the user should check the editor's counter.
- One niche. No stacks of unrelated roles, no adjectives ("reliable", "hard-working", "expert", "guru", "ninja"), no emoji, no ALL CAPS.
- Make the three different: one plain keyword title, one with the client type, one with the result.

## 2. Overview

- **The first 200 characters are the hook; the first ~250 are the search preview.** Together they must name the client type, the service or problem, and the single strongest proof on the fact sheet. Don't let the text break mid-thought at 250. Show both counts.
- Never open with the freelancer's name, a greeting, years of experience, excitement, a badge the card already shows, or a question that could sit on any profile.
- After the hook: services → proof → how you work (only if the freelancer said so) → one clear next step on Upwork.
- **One profile now serves every client.** Lead with the main service. Give secondary services one short "I also help with…" line, not equal billing. Upwork reorders the portfolio and work history per client, so each service line needs matching portfolio items, not more overview text.
- Keep claims in sync with the work history and skills.
- Usually 150–300 words. Show the word count.

## 3. Portfolio descriptions

One per item, built as **problem → what I built → result**. This is Upwork's own Situation–Task–Action–Result advice, compressed.

- **Title, 40 characters or fewer:** names the deliverable and the result or domain ("EV charging app for iOS and Android"), not "Project 1".
- **Description:** the first 300 characters stand alone with the problem and the result; 600 characters or fewer in total. Problem: what the client needed or what was broken. What I built: the freelancer's part, the main tools, and one decision or trade-off. Result: from the fact sheet (users, revenue, funding, speed, a review quote), or `[ADD: result]`.
- **Role field** (up to 100 characters): the freelancer's exact role.
- **Skill tags:** the 5 tags matching that service line.
- Say which items to **link to their Upwork contract** so the client's feedback shows on them. Linked contracts are the hardest-to-fake proof there is.
- Put the item that best matches the main service first. Aim for 3–5 items per main service. Name items to remove because they don't match the niche, and say whether a labelled sample project could fill a gap.

## 4. Skills

- **15 ranked core skills**, then up to **5 extra** marked "add if your editor allows 20". Upwork's own articles disagree on 15 versus 20; say so in one line.
- Order for search: the main service noun first, then the tools and services clients in that niche search for, then supporting skills. The editor's order is the display order.
- Only skills the profile shows real work in. Cover each service line's main search terms.
- Remove duplicates ("iOS" next to "iOS Development") and other-niche skills. List what was removed and why.

## 5. Proposal template

- **120–250 words** by default; under 80 for simple jobs. English.
- **Line 1 (about 200 characters or fewer, the part clients see in their list):** the client's problem in their words plus one matching proof. `[ADD: the client's goal in their words]` + `[ADD: the most relevant result from your profile]`.
- **Greeting:** "Hi [ADD: client name]" if known, else "Hi". Never "Dear Hiring Manager" or "To Whom It May Concern".
- **Body:** one quantified similar result that points to the matching portfolio item (the proposal's portfolio highlight); 2–3 lines of approach naming the tools and one specific detail from the post.
- **End:** one sharp question about their project, and one low-effort next step (a short call or a small paid test).
- `[ADD: code word]` line for jobs that ask for one.
- The niche claim must match the new title and the linked portfolio item. Flag any mismatch.
- **Usage notes under the template:**
  - Apply early.
  - Bid inside the posted budget or say why not.
  - Answer every screening question specifically.
  - If you use AI, edit the draft heavily before sending.
  - Boosting buys a top slot, not a better pitch.

## 6. Newcomers and non-US freelancers

- **No Upwork reviews yet:** lead with substitutes for track record. Use off-Upwork results, a portfolio with outcomes, clearly labelled sample projects and checkable certifications. Never hide that they're new; never fake a history.
- **Outside the client's region:** state working-hours overlap and English level plainly, once.
